How one part gets drawn

Quick, one-at-a-time jobs are the point, not the problem.

  1. Send it

    The broken part, the pieces of it, or photos with a tape measure in frame. The part it mates with, if it can travel.

  2. Measure

    Every dimension that matters. The ones worn away get worked out from what they fit.

  3. Model

    A 3D model in {{ config.extra.cad }}, checked against the parts around it.

  4. Draw

    A dimensioned drawing, yours to keep, so the next one is a phone call, not a project.

  5. Prove, if it matters

    Where fit is critical, a plastic prototype first, printed by you or a local printer. It's cheap to be wrong in plastic.

  6. Make

    Once it's paid for, take the files to your favorite machine shop. Or have a small number made here.

Why a small shop wants the one-off

Most established shops want long production runs that keep their people busy for months. A single replacement part is an interruption to them. Here it's the job: variable work, which is the interesting kind.

Oil and gas operations, manufacturing plants, mines and farms all run machines that have outlived the companies that built them. Mark spent years in mining and industrial service, and in agricultural technology, before opening this shop.
